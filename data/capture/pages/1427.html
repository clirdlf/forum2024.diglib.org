The Local Guide

Welcome to the Local Guide for East Lansing, Michigan, the city where the in-person DLF Forum is happening! Big thank you to the 2024 DLF Forum Community Committee for creating this guide. Keep in mind this guide is not exhaustive and there might be unforeseen issues such as construction, so please be sure to check the navigation before heading out to any of these suggestions.

Where to Eat

Campus/Surrounding East Lansing
East Lansing/Okemos
Lansing
Campus/Surrounding East Lansing

These spots are less than 1 mile from campus.

  • Aloha Cookin’: Hawaiian food near campus (closed Mondays and Tuesdays)
  • BAPS Home Style Korean BBQ: $20-30 per person, A KBBQ restaurant that has an assortment of Korean dishes. Their lunch specials are a deal less than $15 for a filling meal.
  • Campus dining halls: Brody (flagship), Akers, Case Hall South Pointe have the most variety. Compare daily menus, most are all-you-care-to-eat. Venues are accessible via elevator or on a ground-level floor, menus provide options for vegetarian, gluten-free, Kosher, Halal and other dietary needs. Public dining rates: Breakfast: $9 plus tax; Lunch or Dinner: $13 plus tax; Children (ages 2-9 years): $6.75 plus tax
  • Charlie Kang’s: $10-20 per person, Chinese and Korean cuisine with a wide range of flavors, near campus.
  • Crossroads Food Court at the International Center: $1-20 per person. A food court featuring Subway, Panda Express, Woody’s Oasis Mediterranean Deli, and the Roost
  • El Azteco: $10-20 per person, casual Mexican food with a rooftop dining area in business since 1976.
  • For Crepe Sake: $10-20 per person, near campus this breakfast and lunch spot has evening service Thursday to Saturday. Their menu has sweet and savory crepes, Belgian waffles, fruit smoothies and more.
  • Playa Bowls: $10-20 per person. A health food restaurant chain.
  • Sapporo Ramen & Noodle Bar: $10-20 per person, cozy ramen bar offering vegetarian options.
  • State Room (Kellogg Center): $30-50 per person. High-end dining. They have all-you-can-eat buffets M-F for $14 (plus tax) and a la carte dinner.
East Lansing/Okemos
  • Black-owned restaurants (curated list by Lansing Foodies)
  • Coral Gables: $10-20 per person, originally built in the 1920s as a roadhouse is now a family restaurant with an assortment of Americana food and private meeting rooms.
  • Cottage Inn: $10-20 per person. A midwestern chain serving deep-dish and thin-crust pizza, salads, etc.
  • Bell’s Greek Pizza: $1-10 per person, this pizza joint has been in East Lansing since 1967. The menu has pizza, pasta and other Mediterranean food.
  • Everyday Authentic Chinese Cuisine: $20-30 per person, serves Cantonese style food and some American Chinese favorites.
  • Georgio’s Pizza: $1-10 per person, small chain pizzeria offering unique pizza styles.
  • Grand Traverse Pie Company
  • Korea House Restaurant
  • Pizza House: $10-20 per person. A family-owned local pizza chain with locations in East Lansing and Ann Arbor for Chicago-style pizza.
  • Sindhu Indian Cuisine: $10-20 per person, North and South Indian food, family run with many vegetarian options. They have buffets on Sundays
  • Sree Saffron: $10-20 per person, Indian cuisine (near Sindhu in the Hannah Plaza)
  • Thai Princess (Okemos): $20-30 per person, variety of Thai specialties. A local favorite for Thai food.
  • Tabooli Mediterranean
  • Woody’s Oasis: $10-20 per person, casual Mediterranean food with many options, been in East Lansing for over 35
Lansing

Frandor / Eastside

  • Altu’s Ethiopian Cuisine & Bar: $10-20 per person, locally owned informal eatery with a variety of meat and vegetarian Ethiopian dishes.
  • Jersey Giant Subs: Up to $10 per person, an East Lansing family-owned sub restaurant since 1979. Offers a wide variety of sub sandwiches. Many locations around the area.
  • People’s Kitchen: $20-30 per person, comfort foods & craft cocktails served amid colorful industrial decor, plus weekend brunch.
  • Ruckus Ramen: $10-20 per person, unique take on East Asian cuisine located within The Avenue bar on the Eastside of Lansing.
  • Soup Spoon Cafe: $10-20 per person, full service restaurant and bar known for their ‘scratch’ kitchen and craft cocktails.


Downtown

  • EnVie: $20-30 per person, a bistro in the heart of Lansing’s downtown area with a rotating menu. Open Tuesday through Saturday for dinner only.
  • Golden Harvest: $10-20 per person, legendary grungy breakfast joint in Lansing. Cash only.
  • Lansing Shuffle: Food hall with ~7 different cuisines represented, on the Grand River in downtown Lansing


Old Town

  • Meat Southern BBQ and Carnivore Cuisine: $10-20 per person, local BBQ joint with kitschy interior. There can be a wait.
  • The Cosmos: $10-20 per person. Wood-fired pizza with a connected full bar, Zoobie’s Old Town Tavern, in Lansing’s Old Town neighborhood.


Westside Lansing

  • Horrocks Farm Market: $10-20 per person, has gelato, beer, wine bar, sushi and other pre-made food. Located within Lansing’s quintessential farm market. Started in 1959 as a road-side fruit stand, this market has grown and evolved over the years. Has a beer garden nestled in their outdoor plant market which often hosts local events.
  • Ohana Sushi: $30-40 per person, all-you-can-eat sushi made with fresh and, when possible, local ingredients.

Where to Drink

Coffee
Bars and Breweries
Coffee

Find these great coffee spots in downtown East Lansing.

  • Foster Coffee Company: a Michigan-based coffee shop is housed within a modern/industrial design space.
  • Haraz Coffee House: Yemeni coffee shop and bakery with locations across the country.
  • Koala Tea & Coffee: Milk tea and bubble tea with baked goods. Their Okemos location has a robot that will deliver your food to you.
  • Moka & Co.: Yemeni specialty coffee shop with incredible baked goods.
  • Strange Matter: Woman-owned local coffee bar and bakery that is very active in the community. There are three locations in the area, with the closest being in MSU’s Union building. Known for their donuts and pastries, their vegan gluten-free donuts are beloved.
Bars and Breweries
  • Beggar’s Banquet: $$, local American food restaurant and pub founded in 1973
  • Crunchy’s Burgers and Beers: $10-20 per person. Local American food restaurant and pub founded in 1982. Home to one of the area’s best burgers and its specialty “bucket of beer”.
  • Hopcat: $10-20 per person. A trendy Michigan-based chain bar
  • Jolly Pumpkin: $10-20 per person. Known for their sour beer. Serves burgers, pizza, and other pub staples.
  • Peanut Barrel: $$, local American food restaurant and pub founded in 1973
  • The Avenue: Set in the heart of Lansing’s eastside neighborhood, The Ave is home to Ruckus Ramen, pinball, pool and live music. LGBTQ+ safe space with mocktail options.

Explore Lansing

Where to Visit
What to Do
Where to Visit

Broad Museum: Housed in a spectacular building designed by Pritzker Prize-winning “starchitect” Zaha Hadid, the MSU Broad Museum boasts collection strengths in Greek and Roman antiquities; Mesoamerican sculptures and vessels; North American and European sculpture, prints, photographs, and paintings; and works in a variety of media from different African, Asian, Latin American, and Middle Eastern cultures. These collections are showcased in the CORE (Center for Object Research and Engagement), which offers unique opportunities to view, reflect on, and investigate art objects spanning 5,000 years of human history. Hours 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM, Wednesday-Sunday; admission free with online registration, available here: https://broadmuseum.msu.edu/visit/getting-here/ 

Garden tour – botanical, children’s: The Beal Botanical Garden offers free tours open to the public. Established in 1873, the W. J. Beal Botanical Garden is the oldest, continuously operated university botanical garden in the United States and illuminates the interconnectedness between people, plants, and place. This living laboratory encourages learning and exploration of the natural world.

Dairy Store: ​​Enjoy ice cream and cheese at the MSU Dairy Store, open Tuesdays-Sundays from 12-8 p.m.

Capitol tour: The Michigan Capitol is open to visitors Monday through Friday. Guided tours are available between 9:00 and 4:00.

Old Town: Old town offers unique experiences through special events and eclectic shops.

Curious Book Shop: The thinking person’s visit to East Lansing wouldn’t be complete without a browse through the three floors of new and used titles at Curious! Check out their layout at http://www.curiousbooks.com/layout.html and make a special effort to get there if you’re into Oziana, rare sci-fi and fantasy, or vintage magazines. After all, you’ll need something to read on the plane home, right? Open Sunday 12 PM – 5 PM, Monday-Saturday 10 AM – 7 PM, and yes, they validate parking!

Potter Park Zoo: This AZA accredited zoo has been around since 1920 and sits on the Lansing River Trail. Known for its otter exhibit and conservation education, Potter Park Zoo is a beloved part of the community. 

Abrams Planetarium: Shows for the public are on Friday and Saturday nights and Sunday afternoons

Note that the MSU Museum will be closed for 18 months beginning in July.

What to Do

Bike tours (MSU Bikes and Renova Bikes): Enjoy the Lansing River trail which goes through MSU’s campus on a bicycle. A great way to explore the area as you follow the Red Cedar River and Grand River as it meanders through East Lansing and Lansing.

 

Lansing Lugnuts baseball game: Lansing’s very own minor league baseball team. The stadium has outfield lawn seats which but up against a kid’s play area, making baseball games fun for the whole family.

 

The Robin Theatre: Located in Lansing’s historical REO town, this local theatre/book store hosts something for everyone. The theatre is located within a 100-year old store front and provides an intimate environment for enjoying an eclectic array of performances, from film festivals to poetry readings.


Bands in Town: Find out about live music happenings in town. Includes local and touring musicians.
